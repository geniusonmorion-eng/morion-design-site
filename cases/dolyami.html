Dolyami x Lamoda - mobile app

About the project

Dolyami — BNPL service from T-Bank. Divides the purchase into 4 equal payments every 2 weeks, without interest. Works in checkouts of Lamoda, Golden Apple, Letual and other stores.

Problem

Users see the “Pay with Dolyami” button, but are afraid to click. Triggers: “this is a loan”, “there will be hidden interest”, “will ruin your credit history”. High drop-off at the stage of choosing a payment method.

Task

Rethink the widget in the Lamoda checkout and the first onboarding screen. Goal: the user understands in 3 seconds - this is not a loan, it’s free, it’s fast.

I started by analyzing real user reviews of Dolyami. I identified three key fears:

Creditophobia “I don’t want to get involved with loans for sneakers. What if this goes to the credit bureau?”

Complexity “Now they’ll force you to take a photo of your passport and fill out a form... It’s easier to pay with a card right away.”

Forgetfulness - “What if I forget when the next payment is? Will I be fined?”

Separately, I recorded: after the introduction of the service fee, negativity in reviews increased sharply. Users perceive it as a hidden overpayment and go to competitors who promise “0% and no commissions.”

I identified 3 segments in relation to money and risks:

Skeptic — looks for a catch, reads reviews, and worries about hidden charges. If something feels like a loan or commission, they leave and return only after re-checking the terms. Main goal: to pay in parts without feeling in debt.

Controller - a pragmatist who plans expenses. They have the money, but do not want to take the full amount out of circulation at once. They use the service as a budgeting tool and compare scenarios: if Dolyami charges a fee and competitors do not, they choose the more profitable option.

Impulsive — makes decisions quickly, focuses on convenience and emotions. Any delay, complicated processing or passport requirement destroys the purchasing impulse. The frequency depends on triggers: holidays, promotions, new products.

For each segment I formulated a Job Story:

I analyzed the BNPL flow of 5 platforms: Lamoda (current), Yandex Market/Split, Letual, Golden Apple, Avito/Plait. For each - the full path from the product card to payment with annotations.

Key Findings:

Yandex Split shows the commission in rubles, and not as a percentage - this reduces anxiety. The toggle switch allows you to enable Split directly in the cart, without going to checkout.

Golden Apple sells the entire BNPL category, not a specific service. At the point of comparison, against the backdrop of Split and Podeli, which promise “no interest or overpayments,” the signature of Dolyami “a service fee is possible” looks like a warning about the threat.

Letual uses accordion mechanics - 4 payments are immediately visible in the list of payment methods.

Avito/Plait minimizes the barrier to entry: the first payment is from 1 ruble, the “0%” badge on the free plan.

Conclusion: a new Lamoda user has no rational reasons to choose Dolyami among competitors.

Based on reviews, segmentation and competitive analysis, I formulated 7 product hypotheses linked to metrics and 6 behavioral hypotheses.

Next, I prioritized the hypotheses using the ICE method and identified the key ones for customer development.

I compiled two guides: for active BNPL users (6 blocks of questions) and for avoiders (4 blocks). Conducted in-depth interviews and tested each hypothesis on each respondent.

Image placeholder

Alex (Skeptic) — pays by debit card and follows the principle “I only spend what I have.” Dolyami = “debt trap”, “button for the lazy”. He is not afraid of data checks, but of losing financial discipline. The speed of the flow scares him: “I perceive it as a slot machine button.” The only hypothesis that interested him was earning interest on reserved funds.

Olga (Anxious) - avoids the button and thinks that if she presses it, “they’ll immediately issue a loan.” Dolyami = “microloan”, “fraud”. But she reacts calmly to the phrase “installment plan”. She did not know that the service was from T-Bank; once she found out, her trust grew sharply. She is ready to try it for a small amount (1000₽) as a test drive.

Nastya (Pragmatist) — an active user who buys through Podeli and Split. She uses BNPL not out of necessity, but to buy more items at once. Still, she hides it from friends: “I’m ashamed to say it, it sounds like I’m poor.” She was afraid that the data would go to the credit bureau and affect her mortgage approval.

Price transparency - confirmed by all three. Commissions and conditions are ok, to be honest they are shown right away.

Visual process - the strongest trigger for anxious users: “I’m afraid to click and have a loan issued immediately.” Skeptics also need a clear sense of control.

Anti-bureaucracy — for Nastya and Olga, it removes the main fear of entering. For Alex it is neutral.

Bank brand — critical for Olga: it removes the association with microfinance lenders. For anxious users, trust grows sharply.

Autonomous control - everything is different. We need controlled autonomy: advance notifications, “auto/manual” selection.

Simplicity vs speed - not confirmed for all three. Speed ​​reduces trust. Don’t sell “in 1 click”, but add “smart friction”: steps, checkboxes, confirmation screen.

Solution

Designed a complete flow of Dolyami in three stages:

Start of registration

A compact badge on the product card, a bottom sheet with a payment schedule and removal of fears, an accordion in the cart with a toggle and a T-Bank icon on the CTA to increase trust - three touch points that take the user from interest to payment without leaving the funnel.

Identification

For an authorized user with pre-filled fields - full name, date of birth, email. The subtitle “loan agreement is not drawn up” removes the main fear from the study (7 out of 10 associated the service with a microloan).

Minimum form: one field + button. There are no extra steps - the entry threshold is as low as possible.

For a new user, the same three fields without a passport - the form does not look like a loan app. The same subtitle about the lack of a loan agreement.

Confirmation and payment

Visually, the payment screen is closer to a lifestyle service than to a banking interface - a card with a custom gradient. Payment methods have been redesigned: instead of manually entering details, there are quick buttons AlfaPay, SberPay, SBP and T‑Pay with a transition to the bank app. Manual card entry remains, but as a fallback option, not the main scenario.

Manually entering a card remains as a backup scenario - the service remembers it and shows it among the saved ones the next time you make a purchase, reducing repeated payment to one touch.

Three bottom sheets on top of the payment screen - each is opened by tapping on the corresponding element: the info icon shows an explanation of the service fee in simple language, the “1 product” link reveals the composition of the order with the price, the promotional code field - enter and apply the discount. All additional information is available without leaving the payment screen.

Product findings

Review analysis turned out to be more valuable than I expected. Users’ fears are not about the interface, but about the perception of the category. As long as the widget looks like a bank, no amount of button redesign will help.

The interviews refuted one of the main hypotheses: “simplicity and speed = conversion.” All three respondents said the opposite - the speed of registration raises suspicion, not trust. This expanded the concept: instead of “register in 10 seconds” - visual steps, confirmations, control.

Competitive analysis showed that Dolyami loses not in terms of product, but in terms of communication. In one selection menu, Split and Podeli promise “no interest,” while Dolyami warns of a “possible service fee” - it’s a losing battle before it even begins.

ICE prioritization cut off weak hypotheses at the start. Smart Reserve, social proof, micro-loyalty are interesting ideas, but with a low ICE rate. Without prioritization, I would try to implement everything and blur the focus.
