Overchat AI - login and registration

What is Overchat? What is the essence of the product?

Overchat is a subscription marketplace for AI models. Users choose a tariff (premium, medium, etc.), each of which includes a certain number of requests in the form of points. New users receive 200 free points when they first sign in, which encourages engagement and increases the likelihood of upgrading to a paid subscription.

Problem

More than 2,000 users a month visit the platform, but only 100–300 of them successfully register or log in. Those who do register make an average of 3-4 requests per month and never return. This points to two key problems: high churn at the entry stage and low frequency of use of the service.

Before:

Work plan

Input gathering. Communication with CPO, marketing and development to form a Definition of Ready.

UX audit. Analysis of the current version of registration and login: identifying problems and generating primary hypotheses.

DOR formulation. Capture understanding of the task, context, and measurable criteria for success.

Discovery. Research best practices and search for additional hypotheses.

Prioritization. Formulation of hypotheses and sorting according to the value/complexity formula.

A/B testing. Rolling out to 50% of users - old and new versions in parallel.

After talking with the team, I recorded four key criteria:

Increase in conversion to active user

Increased browsing depth and CR to Active User through improved login and registration.

Increased motivation after registration

Increase CR and Activate Something Rate by demonstrating the value of the product.

Increased engagement

Through customization, multi-format elements and emotional design. Metric: NPS.

Retention growth

Increasing Customer Retention Rate by demonstrating real benefits when introducing new practices.

Here are some results from this stage.

For new users

— Aggressive call for registration: the user cannot explore the product before creating an account.

— The template registration window is no different from competitors, which is why there is a feeling of “just another service.”

— Limited login methods: Gmail and Google only. Most of the audience is cut off.

— Emails with a confirmation code end up in spam — confirmed by 3 out of 8 respondents.

For existing users

— There are no prompts for quickly entering email - the address is entered again manually.

— In the desktop version, attention is distracted due to the many elements in the background.

— The CTA button is located at the top, although on mobile phones it’s more convenient to click at the bottom.

— Availability in the App Store and Play Market is indicated, but the app has not yet been released.

— The confirmation code is not visible in the push header - you need to open the email.

The combination of UX audit and discovery allowed us to generate five hypotheses:

Email Validation

If you add a dropdown to select an email service with a notification about supported providers and a check for bots, we will speed up the process, increase conversion and reduce fraud.

Magic Link

If you implement login via magic link, we will save the user from memorizing and entering data, reduce the time for registration, and reduce the Drop-off Rate.

Email navigation

If you show the user how to find the email with the code (including the Spam folder), we will increase completion conversion and reduce drop-off from the form.

Fullscreen registration

If we switch the login form to full screen, we will reduce the cognitive load and focus the user on the target action. Metric: Conversion Rate.

Demonstrating value

If at the login stage we show the results that the user can achieve inside Overchat (code generation, variety of AI models, detailed images), we will increase the Trial-to-Paid Conversion Rate among new users.

After outlining the hypotheses, we sorted them. All prioritization can be broken down using the conventional formula value/complexity. After which we discussed with the team represented by the CPO & Marketer what we would take in the context of the first iteration.

After discussion with the CPO and the marketer, hypotheses 1, 2, 3 and 4 were taken into the first iteration - technically they were easier to implement. Hypothesis 5 (demonstrating value) was put back in the backlog for the next iteration.

First iteration design

Login and registration form

On the left is a value showcase: examples of what a user will get using Overchat's AI models. A caption about the free trial for new users. The form is displayed on the full screen, distracting elements have been removed.

Code confirmation screen

Together with the development, we solved the problem with spam emails: on the confirmation screen we highlight which email the code was sent to. On the left is a visual instruction of where to go in the email client so that the user does not waste time searching for the email.

UX testing

Before launching the A/B test, we conducted usability testing with a focus group - we drew all the corner cases and checked how the new concept works.

Test results:

— Average registration and login time: 45 seconds (it was 60 seconds). The hypothesis about cognitive load was confirmed - users stumbled at the code entry stage.

- 6 out of 8 respondents confirmed that they did not understand what value they would receive after authorization - confirmation of the hypothesis for the next iteration.

A/B test results (30 days, 50/50)

After a month of testing, we compared the old and new iterations:

+300 new unique users per month - Statistically significant increase in MAU due to reduced cognitive load and emotional design.

High CSAT Score — a survey of satisfaction with the new registration and login showed positive dynamics.

Trial-to-Paid Conversion Rate increased by 1.5 times — more users switch to a paid subscription after the free period.

What's next

Hypothesis 5 remains in the backlog: demonstrating value at the entry stage. Based on the test results, it received additional confirmation: the majority of respondents did not understand why they should register. We plan to implement a results showcase (generating code, images, working with documents) in the next iteration and measure the impact on Trial-to-Paid CR.
